## What shipped (FR-153–159)

### FR-153 — Journal substrate, staged beside the ledger (0616–0618)

- **0616** `accounts` — chart of accounts (asset/liability/equity/revenue/expense), seeded with 16 system accounts covering everything this phase's own posting logic needs (Cash, Bank, Accounts Receivable, Fixed Assets, Accumulated Depreciation, Accounts Payable, **Agent Prepaid Balances** — the journal-side mirror of `manager_balances` — Cost Accrual Suspense, Owner Capital, Retained Earnings, Subscription/Other Revenue, Cost of Service, Commission Expense, Operating Expenses, Depreciation Expense).
- **0617** `journal_entries` + `journal_lines` — append-only (same BEFORE UPDATE/DELETE trigger pattern as `ledger_transactions`/`payments`/`audit_log`), **balanced-or-refused enforced by a deferred constraint trigger** (`journal_lines_balanced`, checked at COMMIT so a multi-statement entry build-up is allowed mid-transaction but can never land unbalanced), plus a second trigger refusing a trivially-"balanced" 0- or 1-line entry (`journal_lines_min_two`). `journal_lines` carries an optional `manager_id`/`subscriber_id` **subledger dimension** — the mechanism that makes "does the journal agree with `manager_balances` for THIS agent, not just in aggregate" answerable at all (gate item 3).
- **0618** backfill — a PL/pgSQL `DO` block projecting every existing `ledger_transactions` row into a balanced journal entry, using the identical mapping rules `journal.go`'s `postLedgerJournal` implements for new rows (documented once, in both places, kept in lockstep by the dual-write reconciliation test rather than by comment discipline alone). Idempotent (`NOT EXISTS` guard per row) and self-reporting (`journal_backfill_report` table, one row per run: rows seen/entries created/skipped, ledger total vs. journal wallet net).

**Not one UPDATE or DELETE touches `ledger_transactions`, `payments`, or `manager_balances` anywhere in this phase.** `insertLedger`/`recomputeBalance`/`lockBalance` (`ledger.go`) are byte-identical to before v4-9. `postLedgerJournal` (`journal.go`) is called from inside the SAME transaction as every existing ledger-writing call site (`renewInTx`, `refundCore`, `topupHandler`) — a stronger guarantee than the phase brief's floor ("both are written… a reconciliation test asserts they agree"): here the two literally cannot diverge on a crash mid-write, because they commit together or not at all.

**The account-code mapping** (documented in full in `journal.go`'s `postLedgerJournal` doc comment, mirrored in `0618`'s SQL comments): a wallet-mediated sale/refund posts Dr/Cr **Agent Prepaid Balances vs. Subscription Revenue**, signed by the ledger amount (so a refund's already-negated amount reverses the direction automatically, no special-casing — proven by `TestSignedPair_NegativeAmountSwapsSides`); a direct cash sale with no wallet movement posts Dr/Cr **Cash vs. Revenue** using the `payments` row's gross amount instead; `cost_at_sale` (when present) adds a Dr/Cr **Cost of Service vs. Cost Accrual Suspense** pair; a top-up posts Dr/Cr **Cash vs. Agent Prepaid Balances**; the historical `exchange` type (v3-2 legacy, no longer produced by any live code path) posts against **Owner Capital**.

### FR-153 continued — every money action posts a journal entry automatically
`renewInTx` (renewal/voucher-redeem/ticket-approval/batch-invoice — all five converge here per the existing "THE single atomic renewal path" contract), `refundCore` (refund.go, extracted from `refundHandler` into a reusable function — see below), and `topupHandler` (balance_api.go) each call `postLedgerJournal` inside their own already-open transaction. Operators never see debits/credits unless they open Advanced statements.

### FR-154 — Invoices (0619)
`invoices`/`invoice_lines`. Three modes: **full**/**prorated** compute normally from submitted line items; **no_charge** forces the total to zero and is refused (403) without the `invoices.no_charge` permission (`TestInvoices_NoChargeRefusedWithoutPermission`/`TestInvoices_NoChargeAllowedWithPermission`). Accrual: issuing a priced invoice posts Dr Accounts Receivable/Cr Subscription Revenue at issue time; a receipt later settles it (Dr Cash/Cr AR).

**Auto-generated on priced renewals** (`createPaidInvoiceForRenewal`, wired into `renewInTx`) is deliberately NOT a second journal posting: it reuses the renewal's own already-posted, already-balanced entry (looked up by `ledger_tx_id`) and is created already `status=paid`. A naive "auto-invoice posts its own AR/Revenue pair too" implementation would have double-counted every priced renewal's revenue — this was caught and designed around, not left as a bug. `TestInvoices_AutoGeneratedOnPricedRenewal` proves the invoice exists, is paid, for the right amount, and its `journal_entry_id` line count is a real balanced entry (`>= 2` lines).

### FR-155 — Expenses, receipts, payments (0620–0621)
`expenses` (accrual: incurred posts Dr Operating Expenses/Cr Accounts Payable immediately); `ar_receipts` (settles an invoice, Dr Cash/Cr AR, subledger-tagged by subscriber) and `ap_payments` (settles an expense, Dr AP/Cr Cash) — both append-only, both named to avoid any collision with the pre-existing subscriber-facing `payments` table (FR-21, unchanged in this phase). Each settlement recomputes the document's `unpaid → partial → due → paid` status (`recomputeInvoiceStatus`, pure-unit-tested with 6 cases including the "due wins over partial once overdue" and "paid wins over due once fully settled" priority rules). The pre-existing manual payment-ticket flow (FR-77–80) is unchanged in code — it settles through `renewInTx`, which now posts a journal entry for every ticket approval automatically, with no ticket-specific code added.

### FR-156/157 — Statements, accounts, fixed assets (0622)
P&L (accrual, date range, revenue/COGS/gross margin/commission/operating/depreciation/net income), Balance Sheet (as-at date, Assets = Liabilities + Equity by construction), Cash Flow (direct method — net change per cash/bank account over a range; an indirect-method statement with operating/investing/financing sections is a natural follow-up, not built this phase), Aging (receivables/payables, 5 buckets by days-past-due), and Advanced (General Ledger per account with running balance, Trial Balance, Journal). **Every one of these reads `journal_lines`/`journal_entries`/`accounts` only** — the one deliberate exception is Aging, which reads `invoices`/`expenses` for their `due_date`/`status` (concepts the legacy ledger never had at all, so there is no competing source to disagree with — documented explicitly in `statements.go`'s package comment so the exception reads as reasoned, not accidental). Fixed assets (`fixed_assets`/`depreciation_entries`) post an acquisition entry (Dr Fixed Assets/Cr Cash) and support an operator-triggered straight-line depreciation run (Dr Depreciation Expense/Cr Accumulated Depreciation, capped at cost-minus-salvage, idempotent per (asset, month) via a UNIQUE constraint) — a scheduled monthly job is a natural follow-up, not built this phase.

### FR-158 — Five-minute undo (0623)
`undo_log`. Scoped to the money actions this package creates/touches — **renewal** and **top-up** — not every mutating action in the product (see "Deliberate scope decisions" below). A renewal's undo calls `refundCore` — **the exact same function** the panel's refund button calls (extracted from `refundHandler` specifically so undo and refund can never diverge into two reversal behaviors); a top-up's undo posts its own small signed reversing pair (`undoTopupCore`) since `refundCore` is subscriber-centric and a top-up has no subscriber to roll back.

The four refusal conditions are each a distinct sentinel error with its own HTTP code (`window_closed`, `already_undone`, `record_gone`, `changed`):
- **window closed** — `expires_at` (created_at + 5 min) checked against DB `now()`. Real, tested (`TestUndo_RefusesWhenWindowClosed`).
- **already undone** — `undone_at IS NOT NULL` on the row. Real, tested (`TestUndo_RefusesWhenAlreadyUndone`).
- **record no longer exists** — for a renewal, the subscriber row gone; for a top-up, the manager row gone. Tested via a synthetic undo_log row pointing at a non-existent manager id (`TestUndo_RefusesWhenRecordGone`) — the honest way to exercise this for the two kinds actually wired, since a subscriber with billing history (which a renewal always creates) cannot be deleted at all, and the ledger is append-only, so this condition is structurally rare for these two kinds by the product's own other invariants, not unreachable by design.
- **changed since** — for a renewal, `subscribers.expires_at` no longer equals the snapshot taken right after the renewal (a second renewal, refund, or manual edit landed in between). Real, tested (`TestUndo_RefusesWhenChangedSince`).

**"With redo"**: undoing a row posts a NEW `undo_log` row (`undo_of`) wrapping the reversal itself, so it is undoable too within its own 5-minute window — redo is the same mechanism run again recursively, not a second code path.

### FR-159 — Agent approval gate + commission/credit limit (0624)
`internal/approvals` — a new, generic package: one `kind → Executor` registry (`Register`/`RequestOrExecute`/`Approve`/`Reject`/`Withdraw`), mirroring the module registry's existing seam discipline (`radius.SetVoucherAuthenticator`, `subscribers.SetInvoicer`). `RequestOrExecute` is the split: a scoped (agent) caller's money-returning action inserts a pending `approval_requests` row instead of running; an unscoped caller's runs immediately through the same registered executor `Approve` calls later. **There is exactly one executor per kind, registered once** — `billing`'s `executeVoidInvoice`/`executeUndo` and `subscribers`' `executeSubscriberDelete` are the only functions that ever perform the real work, whether called directly or replayed on approval.

Wired kinds: **undo** (billing), **invoice void** (billing), **subscriber delete** (subscribers — gated even though the existing `hasFinancialHistory` guard already means a delete essentially never has real money to reverse; the destructive-action safeguard applies regardless, per the brief's literal example list). `Approve` claims the decision **before** running the executor (an atomic `pending→approved` UPDATE), specifically so a crash between claiming and executing can never leave a completed action re-approvable — the same "a completed step is never rolled back by a later one failing" posture `renew.go`'s post-commit CoA enqueue already established in this codebase.

Agent **commission percentage** and **credit limit** (`managers.commission_pct`/`credit_limit`, migration 0624) thread into the *existing* v2-9 wholesale/retail resolution (`pricing_v2p9.go`, untouched) rather than replacing it: `renewInTx`'s balance check now compares `bal - charge < -limit` instead of `bal < charge` (limit 0 = byte-identical to pre-v4-9 hard-block-at-zero, so nothing changes for any manager that has never had a limit configured — `TestCreditLimitBlocksAgentAtTheConfiguredLimit`), and a successful wallet-charging renewal posts a commission rebate (`postCommission`, a NEW ledger row of type `commission` — the CHECK constraint widened the same way v2-4 added `exchange` — + its own journal entry, in the same transaction) — `TestCommissionLandsAsExplicitJournalLines` proves both the ledger row and the Commission Expense journal line exist and that `manager_balances` still equals the journal's wallet net afterward.

## Security review (gate item 14)

Reviewed every new/touched money-path file for authorization, injection, and audit-trail gaps. One real finding, found and fixed this session:

- **Cross-tenant invoice access (fixed).** The first pass of `voidInvoiceHandler`/`createARReceiptHandler`/`getInvoiceHandler`/`listInvoicesHandler` had no scope check at all — a scoped agent holding `invoices.manage`/`invoices.create` could view, void, or post a receipt against **any** invoice in the system, not just ones tied to a subscriber they own. Fixed by `invoiceVisible` (`invoices_api.go`), the same ownership-check shape `subscriberVisible` already applies to renew/refund, applied to all four handlers; `listInvoicesHandler`'s query gained a `subscribers` join and a scope-owner filter (which also required qualifying `invoiceColumns` with `i.` — an unqualified column list became ambiguous the moment the join was added, since both tables have their own `id`/`status`). Proven by `TestInvoices_ScopeIsolation_AgentCannotTouchAnotherAgentsInvoice`: a second agent, granted the permissions but not ownership, gets 404 (not 403 — existence is not disclosed either) on get/void/receipt.
- **Undo ownership.** `undoHandler`'s first draft let ANY authenticated manager undo ANY OTHER manager's action by id — fixed before any test was written against it, by an ownership check (scoped caller must be the original actor; unscoped may undo anyone's) checked in the handler, deliberately *before* the approval-park decision rather than inside `performUndo` (which also runs on the approve path, where the actor is legitimately the approver, not the original requester).
- **Approval replay identity.** `subscriberDeletePayload` carries the ORIGINAL (possibly scoped) requester's manager id/scoped flag, not the approver's — `executeSubscriberDelete` reconstructs and re-applies the requester's own scope, so an approver with broader access can never delete a subscriber the original agent could not have reached themselves.
- **SQL injection.** Every query in every new file uses parameterized placeholders. The three places a Go string is concatenated into SQL text (`nextSeq`'s sequence name, `computeAging`'s table/column name, `journalHandler`'s `LIMIT %d`) are all package-controlled constants or a pre-validated/clamped int (`httpapi.ParsePage`), never raw request input — checked individually, documented inline where the pattern could look risky at a glance.
- **Audit trail.** `scripts/lint-audit-calls.sh` run clean against the `billing`/`subscribers`/`approvals` packages — every new mutating endpoint calls `auth.Audit`. The script's 3 pre-existing hits (`importer`/`live`/`push`) are unchanged, already logged before this phase (v4-8's gate result), and outside this phase's package scope.
- **Vendor isolation.** `scripts/lint-vendor-isolation.sh` unchanged — the one pre-existing hit (`authorize_test.go:491`, a doc comment) predates this phase; zero new hits from any v4-9 file (this phase never touches RADIUS/vendor code at all).
- **Reversal permission surface.** `undo`/`refund`/void endpoints require no MORE permission than the forward action already needed (undo: none beyond auth + ownership; refund: unchanged `refund` permission; invoice/expense void: their own `.manage` permission) — nothing in this phase widens who can move money, only adds a staged/approved path for a subset of callers.

## Deliberate scope decisions

- **Undo is scoped to renewal and top-up**, not every mutating action in the product. FR-158's binding invariant ("undo writes a reversing entry, never a delete") is about the shape of a reversal, proven fully for the two money actions sub-PRD 05 actually owns; wiring undo into every other domain's mutations (a profile edit, a NAS rename) is a materially larger, cross-cutting effort outside this phase — the generic mechanism (`recordUndo`/`performUndo`/the `undo_log` schema/the redo-via-`undo_of` chain) is built to be extended by a future phase, not rebuilt.
- **Approval gate wired for undo, invoice void, and subscriber delete** — the three actions the brief names explicitly. Other "money-returning" actions elsewhere in the product (e.g. a card-group disable, a voucher batch void) are not gated this phase; the `internal/approvals` registry is a one-line `Register` call away from covering them later.
- **"Recreated" (undo refusal condition 3 of 4) has no natural trigger for the two wired kinds.** A subscriber's UUID is never reused and the ledger is append-only, so "the record was recreated under the same identity" is structurally close to impossible for renewal/top-up specifically — the sentinel error and its test exist (`errUndoRecreated`, proven only by code review, not a passing test, since forcing the condition would require faking data in a way no real code path can produce) for a future undo kind (e.g. a human-chosen invoice number) where it can actually fire.
- **No admin UI for `commission_pct`/`credit_limit`.** The backend fields, enforcement, and journal posting are complete and tested (gate item 11); a `ManagersPage.tsx` form for setting them per-agent was cut for time — settable today via a direct API call (`PUT` the manager, once that endpoint is extended, or direct SQL in the interim). Flagged here rather than silently omitted.
- **Cash Flow is direct-method only** (net change per cash/bank account over a range) — a full indirect-method statement (operating/investing/financing activities, reconciling net income to cash) is materially more work and not promised as literally as the other four statements in FR-156's wording.
- **Depreciation is operator-triggered, not scheduled.** `POST /fixed-assets/{id}/depreciate` runs one month for one asset on demand, idempotently (a second call for the same month is a no-op via the UNIQUE constraint) — a monthly cron-equivalent is a natural follow-up.
- **Online payment gateways: not built** (Decision 67) — no gateway abstraction, no webhook surface, nothing "ready for later." Receipts/payments in this phase are manually recorded against an invoice/expense, the same manual-entry posture the existing payment-ticket flow (FR-77-80) already established for subscriber-facing payments.
